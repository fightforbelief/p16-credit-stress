When forecasting the five-year charge-off incidence of a two-year-old loan portfolio, should the reference window include younger, partly observed vintages? We compare additive and multiplicative development in two US Small Business Administration programs. In 68 quarterly vintages per program, recent increments lower mean absolute error by 1.28 percentage points in 7(a) and 0.75 in 504. After excluding every vintage whose donor or target span touches the financial crisis, the 7(a) advantage disappears. In fifteen subsequent vintages per program the additive changes are small and differ in sign. The historical upper forecast covers none of seventeen eligible 7(a) vintages overlapping the financial crisis and seven of thirteen overlapping the pandemic. We also compare original and reconstructed forecasts at three archived reference dates. For 7(a) in September 2020, recent multiplicative factors improve error by 0.185 points with archived inputs but worsen it by 0.215 points with later, date-masked inputs, on identical outcomes. These cases show how window choice and data revision affect a portfolio comparison. They give limited support for transferring a historical ranking to a new cycle.
