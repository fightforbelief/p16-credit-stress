# Design fixed before outcome analysis

Question: at a quarter end, how much does the geometry of incomplete charge-off histories matter for projecting a funded SBA vintage to its twentieth calendar quarter? The unit scored is a program and quarterly first-disbursement cohort. Quarter of disbursement counts as the first interval. Count dated charge-offs per initially funded loan. The primary historical landmark is eight quarters. This is a count incidence, not a dollar loss estimate.

Use the September 2020 FOIA snapshot for a rolling reconstruction covering 1999Q1 disbursements through the last fully observed twenty-quarter cohort. Study 7(a) and 504 separately. Use only dated first disbursements within 365 days of approval, at or before the snapshot date. This makes quarterly denominators comparable across files split by approval year. Count each source row once. Flag charged-off records without usable dates and report the resulting endpoint sensitivity. Do not condition entry on a future terminal status.

Use a trailing eight-quarter equal-vintage mean. Compare carrying forward the early rate, the mean mature five-year rate, a pooled age-to-ultimate development factor, mature-vintage increments applied to today's observed prefix, recent calendar-period increments, and the single freshest observable vintage at each future age. Score equal-vintage mean absolute error, signed error, and worst underprediction. Compare differences with contiguous blocks of eight quarterly vintages, with four- and twelve-quarter sensitivity. Report disbursement cohorts whose post-landmark window overlaps the December 2007 to June 2009 recession separately. Treat these dates as descriptive period labels.

For recent-period forecasts form a one-sided historical margin from the last 20 available mature forecast errors at the same landmark (empirical 90th percentile with finite-rank correction, floored at zero). Report observed nonexceedance and width. Contrast with a doubling and halving of the estimated remaining incidence. Adjacent cohorts overlap in calendar time. Report empirical performance without a nominal coverage guarantee.

Lock the three source editions. In the actual-release evaluation, forecast as recorded in September 2020 and December 2022, then score the same disbursement quarters in September 2025. Use all cohort ages 8 through 19 available at each origin with complete file coverage. For the three snapshots, common target support ends 2019Q3 and begins 2011Q1. For 504 only the 2020 origin is available. Decompose forecast error into changes in the published prefix incidence and error in the unobserved remaining incidence. For the unstratified primary forecast recompute the origin using the final snapshot restricted by event time, where identical training cohort support is available, to quantify the effect of retrospective reconstruction. Report both date masking and true release validation distinctly.

Check sensitivity to calendar widths four and twelve and to program-specific short/long contractual terms (up to 120 vs above 120 months). Regions and demographic characteristics are outside this estimand. Save aggregate triangles, denominators, source hashes and row-exclusion counts to support independent reproduction. Positive, negative and mixed rankings use the same analysis.

Before scoring we added a second multiplicative baseline. At each development age it pools the most recent eight available numerator and denominator counts and chains the resulting age-to-age factors. This distinguishes the effect of recent information from the choice between additive and multiplicative development.
